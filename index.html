Why Stripe, honestly

A business built to keep compounding

Two things convinced me. First, Stripe only earns when its customers do, so the business grows with the whole internet economy: $1.9 trillion processed in 2025, up 34%. Second, it ships the next thing before the current one slows down. Payments, then billing, then banking infrastructure, and this year the rails for agentic commerce: a shared protocol with OpenAI so businesses can sell inside AI assistants, and payment tokens that let an agent buy safely without ever holding your card. Over 700 AI agent startups launched on Stripe last year.

And the personal bit: I read about this space every single day, off my own back, because I find it genuinely fascinating. Working in it, with people who are as interested as I am and hold a higher bar than I've ever been held to, is the whole point of this application.

Your principles, my evidence

I didn't have to change who I am to match these

Stripe's operating principles are public. Here's each one, tested against something I've actually done. No adjectives, just records.

Users firstwork backwards from their needs

I haven't run a business on Stripe, but I've run the kind of business Stripe serves: €50k+ a week through a family restaurant, managed by hand. I know what those owners lose sleep over, and I'll never treat a prospect like a row in Salesforce.

Move with urgency and focusthe world's fastest company

Bartended Manhattan nights and weekends alongside a full-time internship. Shipped a live website and video in one day for a job application. This is the second one.

Curiousinvestigate before being right

I learn by finding the problem first, then building. Once you can define a problem properly, AI makes the solution far clearer. That's how my deal-check agent happened: nobody asked for it, it cut checks from 30 minutes to 5, and my team adopted it.

Resilientsetbacks sharpen skills

Raised €14.5k solo for cancer support through months of cold asks and mostly "no", then ran the Dublin Marathon carrying an injury to close it out (resilience or stubbornness, depending who you ask). 50+ brand activations, cold opening 50 to 100 strangers a shift.

Collaborate egolesslyno fiefdoms, no hoarding

Gave my agent to the team, made it simple enough that they can run it without me, and teach them as I go. At the restaurant, I designed the onboarding checklist that trained my own replacements. The win is the team's number.

Humblenot arrogant or complacent

I don't have an SDR title yet and I'm not pretending otherwise. I'm applying to learn the craft properly, from people better than me.
